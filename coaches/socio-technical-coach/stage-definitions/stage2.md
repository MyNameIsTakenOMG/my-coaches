# Stage 2: Identify Business Capabilities

## 🎯 Purpose

Answer "what must the business be capable of doing" to function—focusing strictly on stable, outcome-based capabilities rather than volatile technical systems, software features, or current organizational chart structures.

## 🛠️ Techniques (Scope Reference)

- Business Capability Mapping & Functional Decomposition
- Capability Taxonomy Modeling
- Socio-Technical Dependency Drafting

## ❓ Core Conversational Targets (Milestones)

_Translate these abstract systemic archetypes dynamically into the specific narrative of the active business context:_

- **Milestone 1 (The Capability Ingestion Target):** Identify the raw, high-level business capabilities required to satisfy the business goals and constraints established in **Stage 1**.
- **Milestone 2 (The Functional Decomposition Target):** Break down a major capability into its supporting child-capabilities to expose hidden dependencies (e.g., decomposing "Intake" into "Identity Verification" and "Eligibility Assessment").
- **Milestone 3 (The Capability Stability Target):** Separate stable, long-term capabilities from highly volatile capabilities that are subject to frequent regulatory changes or operational shifts.
- **Milestone 4 (The Socio-Technical Mapping Target):** Map who (which specific actors or teams identified in Stage 1) owns or executes these capabilities today, revealing operational bottlenecks.

## 🚫 Common Pitfalls (Calibration Triggers)

- **The CRUD/Feature Illusion** ──> Listing shallow data manipulation steps (e.g., Create Customer Record, Edit Dispatch Screen) instead of high-level, stable business capabilities.
- **Org-Chart Mirroring (Conway's Trap)** ──> Mapping capabilities strictly to existing company departments (e.g., "The Ops Team Capability") instead of naming true business functions.
- **The System-Centric Blindspot** ──> Describing a capability by naming a legacy software tool (e.g., "The Excel Spreadsheet Capability") instead of the outcome it achieves.
